## Sensitivity is not a calendar

Duration describes how strongly a bond investment's value may respond to interest-rate changes. FINRA explains that higher duration generally means greater sensitivity. It is distinct from maturity, the date when an individual bond issuer must repay principal. Confusing these ideas can turn a useful risk measure into a misleading promise that an ETF investment will return to its purchase value after a fixed period.

Start with the current fund factsheet and prospectus rather than the word bond in the name. Record the duration, observation date and any methodological definition. Identify the types of bonds held and the other principal risks. Two portfolios with similar duration can have very different credit quality, currencies and liquidity. One shared statistic does not make them interchangeable investments.

## Why prices respond to rates

A fixed coupon becomes less attractive when comparable new bonds offer higher yields. Its market price may fall to compensate a purchaser. When comparable yields fall, an existing fixed coupon may become more attractive and the price may rise. This inverse relationship is a starting point, not a guarantee that every security responds identically to every announcement.

The word comparable matters. An issuer's deteriorating finances can lower a bond price even without a general rate increase. A central bank policy change is not automatically a uniform change in yields at every maturity. Keep the assumed yield shock separate from what was actually observed in the market. Otherwise the scenario can imply more precision than the evidence supports.

## Calculate a simple sensitivity example

A simplified estimate is percentage price change approximately equal to negative duration multiplied by the yield change in percentage points. For duration 6 and a 0.50 percentage-point increase, the estimate is a 3% decline. On a hypothetical $10,000 portfolio, that is a $300 price reduction before income, expenses and other effects. No real fund is represented by these figures.

Units matter. A move from 4.0% to 4.5% is 0.50 percentage points, or 50 basis points. In decimal units, negative 6 multiplied by 0.005 equals negative 0.03, or negative 3%. It is not a 50% shock. State the convention next to the inputs so another reader can reproduce the arithmetic without guessing the units.

For a 0.50 percentage-point decline, the same first-order calculation gives a 3% price increase. These symmetric results are approximations rather than a full bond pricing model. Large changes, options embedded in securities and portfolio changes can make actual behavior different. The output is neither a guaranteed maximum loss nor a complete expected total return.

## An ETF share is not an individual bond

An individual bond has contractual payment terms, subject to default and other risks. An ETF share is an interest in a portfolio, not a promise to repay your purchase price when one underlying bond matures. The fund may replace securities as they mature or trade. An ongoing bond portfolio does not necessarily wind down when today's holdings reach their maturity dates.

Some products have target-maturity or termination designs. Their particular terms require prospectus review and should not be inferred from a short-duration label. Even a specified termination date does not guarantee a particular payout. Review expenses, liquidation provisions and principal risks rather than borrowing the repayment story of an individual bond and applying it to the fund.

## Income does not cancel price risk

A bond fund can distribute cash while its share price falls. Receiving a payment is not proof of a positive total return. Compare beginning value, ending value and distributions using an explicit reinvestment convention. Do not add a quoted annual yield mechanically to a duration estimate and label the sum a forecast. Those numbers may describe different horizons and assumptions.

New market yields can affect future income as the portfolio changes, but timing depends on actual securities and transactions. A trailing distribution measure is different from a forward-looking yield measure. Read the label, calculation window and assumptions. Neither is automatically a contractual annual payment on your ETF shares, and future distributions can differ from earlier payments.

## Risks outside the duration number

FINRA warns that low duration does not mean low risk. Credit risk concerns whether issuers meet obligations. Inflation erodes purchasing power. Call features can alter cash flows. A fund holding difficult-to-trade securities can encounter liquidity pressures. Its exchange price can differ from NAV. None of these risks disappears simply because the portfolio's reported duration is small.

## Preserve assumptions in a review record

Record identity, factsheet date, duration definition, sectors, credit categories, currency policy and principal risks. For each scenario, state the yield change and excluded effects. Display adverse outcomes as clearly as favorable ones. If providers use different definitions, do not claim their duration figures are interchangeable without reconciliation.

This educational framework does not predict rates, rank ETFs or recommend a position. No current portfolio duration is asserted. A personal decision also depends on liquidity needs and tolerance for losses, neither of which can be inferred from a yield headline. Consult appropriately qualified help when the product or consequences remain unclear.
